✝This Month's Word

The God Who Gives What We Don't Deserve

Ephesians 2:4–9

"But God, being rich in mercy, because of the great love with which he loved us, even when we were dead in our trespasses, made us alive together with Christ — by grace you have been saved — and raised us up with him and seated us with him in the heavenly places in Christ Jesus, so that in the coming ages he might show the immeasurable riches of his grace in kindness toward us in Christ Jesus. For by grace you have been saved through faith. And this is not your own doing; it is the gift of God, not a result of works, so that no one may boast."

Grace is not a reward. It is a rescue.

Most men are wired to earn. We earn our paycheck, our reputation, our place at the table. So when we hear "God loves you," we instinctively look for the fine print — what do I have to do to keep it? But that is not grace. That is a transaction.

Paul makes it plain: you were dead. Dead men don't cooperate with their own rescue. They don't clean themselves up first. God moved toward us while we were still in our mess — not after we improved, not after we got consistent, not after we stopped failing the people we love. While we were dead.

The Greek word is charis — a gift freely given with no expectation of repayment. It is not tolerance. It is not God lowering the bar. It is God meeting the full weight of the bar himself in Christ and then handing you the result as a gift.

This should wreck your pride and free you at the same time. You cannot boast about your standing before God, because you did not build it. You also cannot lose it by failing, because you did not earn it. It rests entirely on what Christ did — finished, done, permanent.

Brothers, stop trying to pay back what was never a loan.
